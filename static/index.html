About the model

i. Training data

Five wells of the SEG 2016 machine learning contest dataset (2,252 depth samples) were used for training. Two more wells, NEWBY and SHANKLE (912 samples), were kept apart for the final test. Wells without a PE log, and one set of samples from a single facies, were left out. The contest data is not openly licensed, so it is not redistributed here: the example wells are synthetic.

ii. Inputs and features

Seven inputs per sample: GR, ILD_log10, DeltaPHI, PHIND, PE, NM_M (non-marine / marine indicator) and RELPOS (relative position in the formation). Extreme values are capped at the training limits, and each log adds its gradient and a 5-sample moving average: 17 features.

iii. Neural network

Two hidden layers (64 and 32 units, dropout 0.3), trained in Keras and exported to ONNX (15.5 kB). The server runs it with ONNX Runtime, without TensorFlow. The output is a probability for each of the nine facies.

In cross-validation over the training wells the three trained models scored alike (macro F1 0.31 to 0.34); the network was chosen because it runs as a small ONNX file. Full analysis in the notebook.

Current limitations

  • Trained on five wells of a single dataset: most reliable in rocks and logs similar to those wells.
  • Needs NM_M and RELPOS, two interpretations specific to this dataset that most wells do not have.
  • Knows only these nine facies. Rare facies are poorly recognised (on the test wells, 16 % of SS and 6 % of MS samples).
  • About half of the test samples are misclassified, mostly as a neighbouring facies.
  • Accepts CSV files only, with the column names above (a few aliases such as DEPT or PEF are recognised).

What's next

  • More training data: more wells, and wells from other basins, to make the model less dependent on one field.
  • A model for standard logs: a version that needs neither NM_M nor RELPOS, and copes with a missing log such as PE.
  • Better use of depth: models that read the log sequence along depth, and weighting of rare facies.
  • Easier input: a column-matching step for any column names, and LAS files, the standard well-log format.
